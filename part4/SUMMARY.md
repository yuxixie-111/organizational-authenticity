# Did the website get ahead of the practice?

The question was whether inclusion on the About page and the DEI practice in the proxy came apart around 2020, and whether that showed up in the authenticity score. The windows were set before the pages were scored: 2018 and 2019 as the baseline, 2020 and 2021 as the shock, 2022 on its own, and 2023 and 2024 as the backlash. A firm counts only if it has a readable year in both windows. All three checks fail.

From 2018 and 2019 to 2020 and 2021, among 25 firms, featured inclusion on the About page falls by 0.06. The DEI practice in the proxy rises by 0.20. In that window the practice moved. The public page did not.

Year by year, the website names inclusion on 27% of pages in 2020, 41% in 2021, and 52% in 2022, then about 39%. Pages where inclusion is actually a main theme peak in 2022, at about a quarter. That year was left out of the shock window on purpose. The proxy's jump is the 2021 season, and the checkbox stays near 94% after that. The website's smaller wave shows up as the filing's wave is already in the document, and usually as a mention rather than the point of the page.

From 2020 and 2021 to 2023 and 2024, inclusion's share on technology and consumer pages rises by 0.02 (11 firms). Energy is flat (4 firms). Healthcare rises. Four energy firms is too thin to treat as a sector pattern.

Firms whose talk rose more than their practice were supposed to be the ones whose score fell. Across the same 25 firms the correlation is +0.23, the wrong direction. The score rises into 2021, while both halves are rising, and eases in 2023 because the match between featured themes and practices eases. What this sample does not show is a 2020 wave of inclusion language with nothing behind it.

Every firm lived through the same news, so this is about timing, not about what one protest or one court decision caused. In these 50 companies the practice in the proxy moves first and stays. The About page moves less, and later.
