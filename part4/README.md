# Part 4: Did the 2020 values surge decouple from the practice?

## The question

The interesting use of this panel is not another average. It is whether the inclusion language firms added for the public, and the inclusion practice they put in the proxy, moved together.

Two outside events pull in opposite directions. In 2020, protests and a new SEC human-capital rule pushed firms to talk about race and workforce. From 2023, the Supreme Court's decision in SFFA v. Harvard and a run of state anti-ESG laws pushed some of them to talk less. If the public page and the proxy move together, the authenticity index should be quiet. If the public page jumps and the practice does not, the index should fall, which is what a decoupling measure is for.

## Windows, set from the history and not from the chart

- **Pre:** 2018 and 2019
- **Shock:** 2020 and 2021, covering the protests, the pandemic, and the first proxy season that could be rewritten afterward
- **Transition:** 2022, the energy shock and the start of the organized anti-ESG campaign, reported on its own and not folded into either contrast
- **Backlash:** 2023 and 2024

A firm enters a contrast only when it has at least one non-missing observation in each window. The contrast is a within-firm change, so a firm that always talked a lot about inclusion does not create the result by sitting in the average.

## Three statements that can fail

**H1.** From pre to shock, the within-firm rise in *emphasized* inclusion on the About page is larger than the within-firm rise in an operational DEI signal in the proxy. Both are on a 0/1 scale, so they can be compared. Emphasized means the theme is at least 12% of the About page's rubric hits. Operational means a representation figure, a pay-equity statement, or a diversity metric in pay.

**H2.** From shock to backlash, the About page's inclusion *share* falls more for technology and consumer firms than for energy firms. Those are the firms whose customers and employees could see the page.

**H3.** Firms whose stated inclusion rose more than their operational signal, from pre to shock, are the firms whose authenticity index fell over the same step. The test is a correlation, not a median split.

Levels by year are reported next to the within-firm changes. A change that is common to every firm can be invisible in a within-firm design and obvious in the cross-section. Part 2 already suggests the proxy's big inclusion move is the 2021 season, not the spring of 2020. This part asks whether the About page moved on a different clock.

## Result

None of the three checks holds. From 2018 and 2019 to 2020 and 2021, featured inclusion on the About page falls by 0.06 within firm, while the operational DEI flag rises by 0.20 (25 firms). From 2020 and 2021 to 2023 and 2024, the inclusion share rises by 0.02 for technology and consumer firms (11 firms) and is flat for energy (4 firms). The correlation between a larger talk-minus-practice gap and the change in the index is +0.23. The year-by-year counts put the About page's inclusion peak in 2022, the year left out of both comparisons. The reading is in `SUMMARY.md`.

## What this is not

It is not a causal estimate. Every large firm was exposed to the same news. There is no untreated control group inside the S&P 500. The windows are wide on purpose: a single year is a noisy snapshot of a page that may have been rewritten in November. The claim is about timing and about which side of the firm moved, not about the effect of one protest or one court decision.

## What more time would change

Date each About-page change to the month of the snapshot and each proxy to its filing date, and put the two on one timeline. Read the firms with the largest decoupling gap by hand. Add the rest of the S&P 500 so the sector contrasts are not ten firms apiece.
